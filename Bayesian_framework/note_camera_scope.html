Camera corroboration: pooled across sites, or matched to the site?

Written by Bayesian_framework/note_camera_scope.R on 03 October 2026. Every number is computed from the prediction CSV and the posteriors.

The two rules

The deterministic analysis (serra_marin_link_classification_clean.R, and the Sankey app that reads it) builds interaction_id as plant___pollinator, with no site in it. A link is flagged have evidence when the cameras recorded that species pair anywhere in the six sites.

The Bayesian analysis (bayesian_empirical.R) joins the camera records by (plant, pollinator, focal_site), so corroboration means the cameras recorded the pair at that site, and a pair outside that site's camera grid is marked as not watched rather than as not recorded.

Across the 1,624 candidate links, the pooled rule flags 432 and the site-matched rule 262. So 39% of the current flags come from a camera record at a different site.

The two headline numbers. "Cameras confirmed 8.5% of forbidden and 16.3% of phantom links" are pooled values. Matched to the site they become 4.8% and 10.6%, or 10.0% and 14.9% among the links the cameras watched there.

Which rule is right

It depends on the claim, and the two claims differ.

  • Site-level categories need site-matched evidence. Locally unique, locally absent, recurrent and model-elusive all assert something about the focal site. A camera record at another site does not corroborate "this link is realised here". The overstatement is largest for locally absent (47.8% pooled against 14.7% matched) and model-elusive (81.2% against 43.8%).
  • Feasibility claims can use the pooled record. For phantom and possibly forbidden the question is whether the interaction can occur at all, so a record anywhere does bear on it. That reading is legitimate as long as it is called regional feasibility rather than corroboration of the local label.
A caveat that sharpens under the site-matched rule. 673 of the 1,624 links were never watched at their own site, because one partner is missing from that site's camera data. Under the pooled rule this is invisible. Under the site-matched rule, "no evidence" mixes "watched and not recorded" with "never watched", which is why rates among watched links are the ones to report. Coverage also varies between sites:
